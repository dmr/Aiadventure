# Storytelling & narrative copy

Good copy isn't decoration — it's a **promise of transformation** told so the
reader sees themselves in it. Boring copy lists what a thing *is*. Strong copy
opens a gap (tension) and points at who the reader could *become*.

## The S.H.A.R.P. framework

Run any line through these five. The best copy hits 3–5; weak copy hits 0–1.

1. **Stakes** — Open with tension, a cost, or a question that itches. Not
   "Welcome to X" but "Most people do X wrong — and blame the tool." If nothing
   is at risk, there's no reason to read on.
2. **Hero = the reader** — The protagonist is "you", not the product. Write what
   *they* will do, feel, or become. The product is the mentor, never the star.
3. **Anchored in the concrete** — Specifics out-pull abstractions. "A Friday
   16:30 prod hotfix" beats "real-world scenarios". Concrete detail is what makes
   a claim believable.
4. **Restraint (anti-hype)** — Especially for skeptics: undersell, don't oversell.
   Admit the hard parts. Honesty *is* the hook. Cut adjectives; let nouns and
   verbs carry weight. No "revolutionary", "seamless", "powerful".
5. **Promise a shift** — Name the before→after. "From typing to reviewing." "From
   hoping to steering." The reader should finish the line knowing what changes.

## Craft rules

- **One idea per line.** If a sentence has two thoughts, split or cut.
- **Rhythm.** Vary length. A long setup, then a short punch. The full stop is a
  drumbeat — use it.
- **Verbs over adjectives.** "It bites you" > "it can be problematic."
- **Show, don't claim the emotion.** Don't write "exciting" — write the thing
  that *is* exciting and let the reader feel it.
- **Earn every word.** Read it aloud. Delete anything you'd never say to a person.
- **Lean on a live metaphor** if the brand already has one (a campfire → war
  stories told around it), but only if it carries meaning, not just vibe.

## Process

1. Write the **goal** in one plain sentence: what should the reader believe or do
   after reading?
2. Name the **tension** and the **shift** (before → after).
3. Draft 3 variants in different registers (provocative / curious / honest-blunt).
4. Score each against S.H.A.R.P.; keep the strongest, then cut 20% of the words.
5. Read aloud. If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it as if telling one person.

## Before → After

- ❌ "Welcome to our cozy little place. Stay as long as you like." *(no stakes,
  product-centric, vague — could describe anything)*
- ✅ "Most people use AI coding wrong — then blame the tool. In 20 minutes, learn
  when it's brilliant, when it bites, and how to tell the difference." *(stakes,
  reader-hero, concrete time, anti-hype honesty, clear shift)*

## Anti-patterns

- Greeting-as-headline ("Welcome to…", "Hi there!").
- Feature lists pretending to be a story.
- Hype adjectives doing the work a concrete noun should do.
- Talking about the product instead of the reader's change.
- A metaphor chosen for mood that says nothing about the value.
